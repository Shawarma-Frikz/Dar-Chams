01
Idea

A door left open to the sun

The charter turns one sentence into a look: a house in Tunis that thinks about the sun before it thinks about anything else.

NameDar Chams

Dar is house and Chams is sun, in the Tunisian spelling. The name is used in Latin letters on screen, with the Arabic form دار شمس as a quiet second line wherever there is room.

MarkDoorway and disc

An arched doorway, as seen in Tunisian houses, framing a sun disc. The disc sits exactly on the spring line of the arch, where the curve starts. It is the only place the mark uses saffron.

RuleSaffron means energy

Production is saffron. Nothing else is: not a button, not a badge, not a highlight. When a viewer sees saffron they are looking at sun power.

Personality

PreciseCalmWarm without cuteEvidence firstLocal

Think of an instrument panel in a well-kept house: clear labels, honest numbers, nothing blinking without a reason. It should feel like it was drawn for Tunis, not exported from a template.

Choices we refuse

These are the habits that make a generated interface look generated. Each one has a replacement.

  1. The purple-to-blue gradient and glowing glass cardsFlat colour on a cool grey-green ground, with one dark arch band per page.
  2. A sun with a ring of rays as the logoA plain disc in a doorway. Rays are only drawn as data: a sun path, an angle, a ray in the 3D scene.
  3. Emoji or stock icons standing in for devicesOne drawn icon set, 1.75 px strokes, in a 24 px grid.
  4. Three equal cards, each with an icon, a heading and two linesLeft-aligned reference pages, tables, rules and a real diagram.
  5. A round gauge with a needle for battery chargeA flat bar with a marker at the cutoff. It reads faster and does not pretend to be more precise than the model.
  6. Cream paper backgrounds and a serif headlineA cool wash and a variable grotesque with a friendly width, set tight.
  7. Copy that says smart, powerful, seamless or AI-poweredPlain statements with a number and a reason.
02
Mark and name

The doorway and the disc

One shape, built on a 24 unit grid so it can be redrawn exactly in code, in a favicon and in a 3D scene.

  • Grid. 24 × 24 units. The arch is 16 units wide, from x 4 to x 20, and 20 tall, from y 2 to y 22.
  • Arch. A half circle of radius 8 on the spring line at y 10, then straight sides down to y 22.
  • Disc. Radius 3.6, which is 0.45 of the arch radius. Centre on the spring line, on the vertical axis.
  • Colour. Arch in doorway blue, disc in saffron. In one-colour use the disc becomes a hole.
  • Clear space. Keep one disc radius, 3.6 units, free on every side.
  • Smallest size. 16 px on screen, 8 mm in print. Below that use the favicon variant, which drops the grid margin.
Why this shape

The arch is a window onto the sky, the disc is the sun at the moment the day begins, and the geometry is plain enough to build in CSS, in SVG, and as a 3D door in the scene.

Rules

Wordmark is Bricolage Grotesque 800, tracking −3.5 %, set as two words with a normal space. Mark height equals the cap height times 2.4. The Arabic line is Noto Kufi Arabic 600 at 55 % of the Latin size and never replaces it on screen.

Do

  • Use the mark on the wash, the surface or the arch band.
  • Redraw it from the grid for any new size.
  • Keep saffron on the disc only.

Do not

  • Add rays, glow, shadows or gradients.
  • Recolour the disc blue or the arch saffron.
  • Place it on a photograph without a solid band.
  • Stretch, rotate or outline it.

Proportion

Most of any screen is ground and ink. Brand blue is a structural colour. The energy colours are small and sharp.

Ground 62 · ink 20 · blue 12 · teal 3 · rose 2 · saffron 1. Saffron stays the smallest area on purpose, so when it appears it means something.

Day and night

The dashboard is read in sunlight on a phone and in a dark room on a laptop, so both themes are designed, not inverted. Night is a deep blue, not black. Saturated colours are lightened, not left alone, so they keep their contrast.

What the numbers forbid

Saffron on the day ground is only 1.8 to 1. So on the light theme saffron is never a text colour or a thin line. Use it as a filled area with an ink label, or switch to saffron line for strokes. Text on a soft tint always uses ink or the matching ink token, never the accent itself.

Flow lines

Flow line thickness follows power: 2 px per 0.25 kW, never below 2 px, never above 18 px. Lines are straight with right-angle bends. Arrowheads are small and sit on the target edge. A line that carries no power is not drawn.

Priority tiers

Tiers are ordered, so they use one ramp from solid to dashed. They are shown with a shape, a number and a fill, and their colour is blue and slate, never an energy colour.

Numbers and units

  • Space before units. A narrow no-break space between a number and its unit: 3.2 kW, 72 %, 1,250 W. Units are never bold.
  • Decimals. A point in English, a comma in French: 3.6 kWh and 3,6 kWh. Thousands use a narrow space in French, a comma in English.
  • Precision. Power in kW with one decimal above 1 kW, in W below. Energy in kWh with one decimal. Charge in whole percent. Temperatures in whole degrees. Forecasts are never shown with more precision than their band.
  • Time. 24 hour clock. Stored in UTC, shown in local time with the label UTC+1 beside the clock.
  • Signs. Battery power is positive when charging and negative when discharging, with the word beside the sign.
06
Layout

One column on a phone, a reading desk on a laptop

Reference pages are left-aligned with strong rules. The dashboard is a set of blocks that reflow, never a fixed canvas.

  • Base unit. 4 px. Gaps are 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 84.
  • Gutter. At least 16 px on every side, plus the phone's safe area. Content width 1,180 px at most; text blocks 74 characters.
  • Shape. Corners are 3 px on chips, 4 px on controls, 6 px on panels. The only large curve is the arch.
  • Lines over boxes. Use rules and spacing to group. A bordered box is for a distinct object such as a device or an alert, not for every paragraph.
  • Breakpoints. 480, 760, 900 and 1,180 px. Four columns fall to two, then one. Tables scroll inside their own frame.
  • Touch. Targets 44 px high at least, with 8 px between neighbours.

Dashboard frames

Phone, one column

Battery first, then production and use, then the next two days, then what was shed. Bottom bar for time control. Nothing needs a second tap to be read.

Laptop, three zones

A slim navigation column, a main area with the day and the loads, and a log column that explains every decision with its reason.

Presentation

One diagram per slide on the deep doorway ground, with the arch cropped at the left edge. Slides use the same tokens as the app.

07
Data display

Charts that show the evidence and the doubt

The numbers here are illustrative, drawn from a simple sun model for a clear 21 December in Tunis so the plates look real. They are not outputs of the project.

A day

  • Direct labels. Name each line on the line, not in a legend. The three series use the three energy colours and nothing else.
  • Night is a band. Hours without sun are shaded from the sun's own altitude, not a fixed clock time.
  • Stroke weights. 2.2 to 2.4 px for data, 1 px for grid. Production is an area with a stroke so it can be read at a glance.
  • Axes. Few ticks, mono labels, the unit once at the top. Battery charge shares the height with power and is labelled.
  • Synthetic data. Any chart of invented or fallback weather carries the chip "synthetic weather" in the chart header.

A forecast with its doubt

Never show a forecast as one confident number. The hatched band is the likely range and it is always drawn. A low day is not painted red; the label says what will happen, for example "Tomorrow is low. Dishwasher-type loads wait for Thursday."

The forecast card also states how sure it is, with a word and a number: "Medium confidence. Band 3.3 to 7.2 kWh."

Rules for every chart

Do

  • Zero-base bars. Print the unit once.
  • Use hatching for uncertain values and solid fill for known ones.
  • Put a table view behind every chart for screen readers.
  • Show the source chip: sim, wokwi or hardware.

Do not

  • Use a red to green scale for good and bad.
  • Draw a pie or a donut for energy.
  • Show a forecast as a bare line with no band.
  • Animate a chart on every refresh.

11
3D twin and the node

The same identity in the scene and on the board

The 3D house, the Wokwi board and the phone screen should feel like one product.

3D scene style

  • Look. Flat shaded, low detail, no textures. Materials are the palette: walls in blue wash and surface, panels in deep doorway, the arched door with its sun disc, windows in saffron when something inside is on.
  • Sun. A saffron disc on a dotted arc. Its position follows the simulated time, and its arc follows the date.
  • Energy. Dashed saffron lines for production, blue for the battery, rose for the use. Line speed follows power but stops at a fixed maximum.
  • Shed devices. A shed appliance's light goes out and its icon takes the dashed outline.
  • Camera. Three presets: front, roof and cutaway. Default is isometric at 35° from above. No free camera on a phone by default.
  • Quality. Low has no shadows and one light. High adds soft shadows. The setting is remembered and the app chooses Low on a weak computer.

12
Voice

Short, plain, with a number and a reason

Every sentence says what happened, why, and what happens next. English and French are first class. Arabic is a stretch language for the name line.

Say

  • "Water heater waits. Tomorrow looks cloudy, so it will run at 12:40."
  • "Battery 72 %. At this load it lasts about 9 hours."
  • "Panels hold position. Moving would use more energy than it gains."
  • "Measured 1.2 kW, expected 1.8 kW."

Avoid

  • "Our smart AI optimises your energy!"
  • "Oops! Something went wrong."
  • "Guaranteed savings" or any promise about a real house.
  • Exclamation marks, emoji, "seamless", "supercharge", "revolutionary".

Things move because the world moved

The sun travels, panels turn, lights go out. Nothing moves for decoration.

  • Reduced motion. When the viewer asks for it, all transitions become instant and the 3D energy lines stop moving, but every value stays visible.
  • No loops. Nothing pulses forever. A new alert appears once and stays.
  • Time control wins. Animations scale with the simulation speed, and stop when paused.
14
Accessibility

Checks before anything ships

Treat each line as a test. Tick it in the pull request.

  • Text contrast 4.5 to 1, large text and controls 3 to 1, checked in both themes.
  • No meaning carried by colour alone: every state has a word and a shape.
  • Every control has a visible focus ring, 2 px, doorway blue, 2 px offset.
  • Targets at least 44 px, with 8 px between them.
  • Everything reachable and usable by keyboard, with a logical order.
  • Every chart has a table version and a one-sentence summary.
  • Live values announce politely, no more than once every 10 seconds.
  • The page works at 200 % zoom and at 320 px width.
  • Reduced motion is respected everywhere, including the 3D scene.
  • Language is set on every block, and Arabic blocks are right to left.
  • 3D has a full 2D alternative with the same information.
  • No flashing faster than three times a second, ever.

Definition of done for a screen

  • Only token colours, no hard-coded hex.
  • Saffron appears only for production.
  • Every state in the state matrix is drawn.
  • Day and night both checked at phone and laptop width.
  • All numbers are in Martian Mono with a spaced unit.
  • The copy has a number and a reason.
  • Source chip present wherever data comes from the node or from fixtures.
  • The accessibility checklist above is ticked.
